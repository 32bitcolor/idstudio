# ADDIE & SAM explained

> Every Tavoro project runs on a **methodology** — the process you follow to take a request from idea to launched learning. Tavoro ships with the two most common ones, ADDIE and SAM, plus a Custom option. Here’s what each is, when to reach for it, and how it shows up in the app.

> **Note.** A methodology is just a shared map of the work — it doesn’t replace your judgment. Pick the one that matches how your team actually operates, and adapt the phases as you go.

## ADDIE — the structured backbone

ADDIE is the classic instructional-design framework: five sequential phases that carry a course from analysis through evaluation. It’s deliberate and thorough, which makes it a strong fit for high-stakes or compliance content where requirements are known up front and sign-off matters at each step.

**Analyze** — Define the problem before designing a thing. Who are the learners, what’s the performance gap, what are the constraints, and what business outcome is this supposed to move? The output is a clear set of learning objectives.

**Design** — Turn objectives into a plan: the structure, sequence, assessment strategy, and look and feel. This is where storyboards and prototypes take shape — the blueprint, not the build.

**Develop** — Build the actual assets — courses, videos, job aids, assessments — from the approved design. Draft, review, revise, repeat until the deliverables are production-ready.

**Implement** — Ship it. Publish to the LMS, run the pilot, train facilitators, and roll out to learners.

**Evaluate** — Measure whether it worked — reactions, learning, behavior change, and business impact — and feed what you learn back into the next iteration.

### Reach for ADDIE when

- Requirements are well understood and unlikely to shift mid-project.
- The content is compliance, regulatory, or otherwise high-stakes and needs an audit trail.
- Multiple stakeholders need formal sign-off at defined checkpoints.
- You’re coordinating a larger team and want clear, phase-based hand-offs.

## SAM — the iterative alternative

SAM (the Successive Approximation Model) trades ADDIE’s long linear march for tight, repeating cycles. Instead of perfecting each phase before moving on, you build small, get feedback fast, and refine — converging on a good product through successive passes. It shines when the problem is fuzzy, speed matters, or stakeholders need to see something tangible before they can tell you what they want.

**Preparation** — A quick information-gathering and alignment kickoff (often a “Savvy Start” brainstorm) to rally the team and background the problem — measured in days, not weeks.

**Iterative Design** — Rapid rounds of design → prototype → review. Each loop sharpens the concept with real stakeholder feedback before anything expensive gets built.

**Iterative Development** — Build in the same looping rhythm — alpha, beta, gold — reviewing and correcting each release rather than saving all the feedback for the end.

### Reach for SAM when

- The requirements are unclear and will emerge as people react to prototypes.
- Speed and early momentum matter more than an exhaustive up-front plan.
- Your SMEs and stakeholders engage best with something concrete in front of them.
- You expect to revise heavily and want feedback baked into every step.

## ADDIE vs. SAM at a glance

**Flow** — ADDIE is linear and sequential; SAM is cyclical and iterative.

**Feedback** — ADDIE gathers it at phase gates; SAM gathers it every loop.

**Best for** — ADDIE for stable, high-stakes work; SAM for fast-moving, evolving work.

**Risk** — ADDIE surfaces problems late; SAM surfaces them early, cheaply.

## How methodology works in Tavoro

When you create a project, you choose ADDIE, SAM, or Custom. Tavoro seeds the matching phases so you start with a real structure instead of a blank page:

**ADDIE** — Analyze · Design · Develop · Implement · Evaluate

**SAM** — Preparation · Iterative Design · Iterative Development

**Custom** — Starts empty — build your own pipeline phase by phase.

1. Create a project and pick a methodology — the phases appear automatically.
2. Work the phases: set each to **Not started**, **In progress**, or **Done**, and give them start/end dates. Add, reorder, or remove phases anytime — the methodology is a starting point, not a cage.
3. Hang deliverables (courses, storyboards, assessments) off the project, route them through SME review cycles, and track milestones and time against the same plan.

> **Tip.** Not sure which to use? Default to **SAM** when you’re still figuring out what the learning should even be, and **ADDIE** once the requirements are locked and sign-off is the name of the game. You can always add phases to either.
